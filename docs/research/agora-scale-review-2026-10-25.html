What the app does

The Agora LA is a free iOS app from a nonprofit that turns podcast listening into active recall. A listener pastes an Apple Podcasts, RSS, or audio link. The app builds a transcript, writes questions grounded in what the episode actually says, and asks each one right after the passage that answers it.

In hands-free mode, which is on by default, the app pauses the episode, reads the question aloud, listens for a spoken answer, grades it generously, reads the feedback, and resumes. Good answers earn points toward levels. There are no Agora accounts, subscriptions, or in-app purchases. Listeners connect their own OpenRouter account and pay their AI provider directly.

How an episode becomes a lesson

  1. Resolve the episodeOn the phone: an Apple Podcasts link goes through iTunes Lookup to the publisher's RSS feed and the episode's MP3.
  2. Get a timed transcriptThe server uses the publisher's <podcast:transcript> when it covers the episode. Otherwise it downloads the MP3 (up to 250 MB), splits it into 8-minute parts with FFmpeg, transcribes three parts at a time with Whisper large-v3, and shifts each part's timestamps onto the full timeline.
  3. Extract ideasThe transcript is split into 3–12 sections by length. A model lists each section's consequential claims, each with a verbatim quote, four sections at a time.
  4. Verify quotesAny idea whose quote does not appear word for word in the transcript is dropped, along with low-importance and duplicate ideas.
  5. Curate questionsOne high-reasoning call writes up to 18 question and answer candidates plus a 100–170 word episode brief. Code then rejects stock wording, weak overlap between answer and evidence, and clustering, and spreads the best questions across the episode.
  6. Schedule check-insEach question is set to play 3 seconds after its last supporting quote ends, measured against real timestamps.

Why timestamps decide the technology

Every later choice follows from this mechanism. Publisher captions are used only when they cover at least 90% of the episode. Server transcription rejects any 8-minute part that comes back short. A transcription service must return reliable timestamps, and a source with approximate timestamps suits an end-of-episode quiz better than check-ins during playback.

Scale assessment

Holds What already scales

  • Job queue. Postgres with SKIP LOCKED, claim tokens, and 2-second heartbeats. Several servers can share work, and a crashed job is requeued after 3 minutes.
  • No central AI bill. Each listener's key pays, so AI spend and provider rate limits are per listener.
  • Safe fetching. User-supplied URLs are DNS-pinned and private network addresses are refused.
  • Credentials. Keys are encrypted with AES-256-GCM in the database and erased when a job completes, fails, or is canceled.

Breaks What fails first, worst first

  1. No sharing across listeners. A thousand listeners importing one episode cause a thousand transcriptions and analyses. The cache key includes title, model, and prompt count, so even one listener's variations miss.
  2. All-or-nothing jobs. When the final quality gate fails, the paid transcription and extraction are discarded, and a retry repeats everything.
  3. Two pipelines. The TypeScript server and the Swift on-phone fallback implement different logic and will drift apart. The Swift one is the live path whenever the cloud is down.
  4. Operations. API and worker share one process. There are no metrics or traces, and listeners get no notification when a job finishes; the app polls.
  5. Onboarding. A new listener needs an OpenRouter account with credits before the first real lesson.

Throughput estimate. About 4–8 minutes per hour-long episode. Each instance runs 2 jobs at a time by default and 4 at most, so roughly 15–60 episodes per hour per instance. Capacity grows by adding instances.

Leftover code: Apple in-app purchase and credit-ledger tables and endpoints remain in the backend, although version 1.0 is free and has no purchases.

What cheap models change

At roughly 5¢ per episode, a capped free tier paid by the project becomes affordable: 10,000 episodes a month would cost about $500. That is a policy change, since the repo's rules currently forbid an Agora-owned AI key. The repo already contains the right quality test, Backend/scripts/compare-curation-models.mjs, which runs candidate models against the real gates.

Python. The best open models are Python-first: NVIDIA NeMo runs Parakeet, Qwen3-ASR ships with its own forced aligner, and WhisperX adds word alignment and pyannote speaker labels. Recommendation

Replace Whisper large-v3 with Qwen3-ASR-1.7B through OpenRouter: the same price and about 25% fewer errors on the leaderboard. Choose Scribe v2 where speaker labels matter. Backend/src/openAIClient.ts line 16 swaps any model whose name lacks "whisper" back to Whisper, so the change needs a small code edit and a check that the provider returns segment timestamps.

Listener's own AI over MCP

The preferred direction: each listener signs in with their own AI account and customizes an agent that talks to The Agora over MCP, running on that listener's own plan.

How a quiz gets made

  1. ConnectThe listener adds Agora's MCP server as a connector in Claude (web or desktop; it then works on mobile) and signs in to their Agora account during the connector's OAuth step.
  2. CustomizeThey install Agora's plugin, which bundles a skill with the connector, and adjust difficulty, question count, style, and topics.
  3. Ask"Quiz me on this episode," with a link, or "prepare quizzes for my backlog."
  4. ReadThe assistant calls get_transcript(episode, page). Agora returns the cached, timed transcript in pages of about 35,000 characters.
  5. WriteThe assistant drafts questions with verbatim quotes and calls save_quiz.
  6. VerifyAgora's server runs the existing gates from promptPipeline.ts: verbatim quotes, answer alignment, spread across the episode, and timing from real timestamps. It returns specific rejections the assistant can fix.
  7. ListenThe iOS app, signed in to the same Agora account, plays the episode with check-ins and grades spoken answers on the device.

Allowed What signing in can mean

  • The listener signs in to Claude or ChatGPT inside the vendor's own app.
  • Inside the connector setup, they sign in to their Agora account through OAuth. Agora receives an account link, never AI credentials.
  • Claude's free plan allows one custom connector; Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise allow more.

Not allowed What it can't mean

  • Agora's app or server signing in to Claude.ai on the listener's behalf to use their plan. Anthropic reserves consumer sign-in for its own apps and bars routing requests through plan credentials.
  • Using a ChatGPT plan from a hosted app. OpenAI's "Sign in with ChatGPT" plan usage is a limited preview for open-source and locally run apps.
  • MCP sampling, where a server borrows the client's model. The 2026-07-28 spec deprecated it, and new implementations should not adopt it.

Background runs

A listener can set up "prepare quizzes for new backlog episodes" as a Claude Code scheduled task that runs in the cloud on their own plan. These tasks also accept an HTTP trigger, but Agora storing a listener's trigger token to fire it is likely barred by Anthropic's terms; get written confirmation before building on it. On ChatGPT, MCP Events let a server send signed webhooks that start tasks in Work chats on web and desktop.

Linking the listener's Agora account

  • Public tools work before sign-in. The first protected call returns 401 with a resource_metadata pointer, Claude shows a Connect card, the listener signs in on Agora's page, and Claude retries the call.
  • Claude requires the redirect https://claude.ai/api/mcp/auth_callback, PKCE with S256, client ID metadata documents or dynamic registration, rotating refresh tokens, a token endpoint that answers within 10 seconds, and read-only or destructive hints on every tool.
  • ChatGPT redirects to https://chatgpt.com/connector_platform_oauth_redirect and also accepts metadata documents or dynamic registration.
  • Offering Sign in with Apple on that page gives the iOS app the same Agora account. App Review guideline 5.1.1(v) then requires account deletion inside the app.

Grading during playback

A check-in needs a grade within seconds, mid-episode, often hands-free. No policy route lets the app call the listener's subscription model, and chat or scheduled runs are far too slow. Grading has to happen on the phone or through a small API key.

Apple describes its on-device model as not designed for world knowledge or advanced reasoning. Comparing a reply against a supplied expected answer is a narrow task that suits it. The app targets iOS 16, so older devices fall back to the heuristic.

Limits of this mode

  • Runs start in a chat or on a schedule the listener sets up. Agora cannot run a one-button import on the listener's plan, which conflicts with the repo's one-button guardrail, so this needs to be a second mode or the guardrail has to change.
  • Question quality depends on the listener's model and plan limits. Server-side gates keep weak questions out but can trigger retries.
  • Claude's web and desktop apps cap a tool result near 150,000 characters and a call at 240 seconds, so transcripts are paged and long work uses job handles.
  • Transcription still has to be paid for. Without a listener API key, Agora pays about 3¢ per hour (shared across listeners), uses publisher transcripts, or transcribes on the phone.
  • ChatGPT's plan eligibility and mobile support for custom MCP servers are unverified, and its directory rules ban "unofficial connectors," a risk for a YouTube tool.
